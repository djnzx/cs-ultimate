Yes. Your list is basically a **Numerical Methods / Numerical Analysis** course, with one introductory topic on **modeling in systems analysis**.

The best public high-quality match is:

## 1. MIT OCW 18.330 — Introduction to Numerical Analysis

This is probably the closest single course.

MIT describes it as covering **root finding, interpolation, approximation of functions, integration, differential equations, and direct/iterative methods in linear algebra**. That matches your Topics 2–6 almost directly. ([MIT OpenCourseWare][1])

Mapping:

| Your topic                                           | Covered by MIT 18.330? |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Метод Гауса, direct linear algebra methods           | Yes                    |
| Iterative methods for linear systems                 | Yes                    |
| Nonlinear equations: bisection, Newton, etc.         | Yes                    |
| Interpolation: Lagrange/Newton-style material        | Yes                    |
| Numerical integration: rectangles/trapezoids/Simpson | Yes                    |
| Modeling in system analysis                          | Only indirectly        |

Course: **MIT OCW 18.330 Introduction to Numerical Analysis**
Best use: as the backbone for your course.

## 2. Stanford CME 108 / MATH 114 — Introduction to Scientific Computing

This is also very close, especially if you want a more engineering/scientific-computing flavor.

The Stanford syllabus says the course studies numerical solutions for mathematical problems such as **linear and nonlinear equations, interpolating functions, approximating derivatives and integrals**, and implementing algorithms. ([Stanford University][2])

Mapping:

| Your topic                       | Covered by Stanford CME 108?                       |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Linear equations                 | Yes                                                |
| Nonlinear equations              | Yes                                                |
| Interpolation                    | Yes                                                |
| Numerical integration            | Yes                                                |
| Algorithm/program implementation | Yes                                                |
| Modeling in system analysis      | Partly, but not as a systems-analysis theory topic |

Course: **Stanford CME 108 / MATH 114 Introduction to Scientific Computing**
Best use: as a Stanford reference syllabus and structure.

---

## 3. MIT OCW 18.335J — Introduction to Numerical Methods

This is a stronger / more advanced version.

MIT describes it as an advanced introduction focused on **accuracy and efficiency**, including dense and sparse numerical linear algebra, iterative methods, floating point arithmetic, backward error analysis, conditioning, and stability. ([MIT OpenCourseWare][3])

Use it if your course should be more rigorous about:

* conditioning;
* stability;
* floating-point errors;
* convergence;
* modern numerical linear algebra.

But for your exact topic list, **18.330 is closer** than 18.335J.

---

## 4. MIT OCW 2.086 — Numerical Computation for Mechanical Engineers

This is another good applied source. Its lecture notes include **integration**, **linear algebra**, and **Newton’s method for nonlinear problems**. ([MIT OpenCourseWare][4])

This is useful if your students are more engineering-oriented and you want practical examples rather than a pure math style.

---

## 5. For Topic 1: Modeling in Systems Analysis

Your **Тема 1** is not purely numerical analysis. It belongs more to **mathematical modeling / system dynamics / systems modeling**.

Good public MIT sources:

### MIT RES.15-004 — System Dynamics: Systems Thinking and Modeling for a Complex World

This gives a compact overview of system dynamics and systems modeling. MIT describes it as a workshop on **systems thinking and modeling for a complex world**. ([MIT OpenCourseWare][5])

### MIT 2.003 / 2.004 — Modeling, Dynamics, and Control / Systems Modeling and Control

MIT 2.003 is the first part of a sequence in **modeling, analysis, and control of dynamic systems**. ([MIT OpenCourseWare][6])
MIT 2.004 includes creating **lumped parameter models expressed as ODEs**, estimating model parameters, and analyzing time-domain/frequency-domain responses. ([MIT OpenCourseWare][7])

These are not exact “system analysis” courses, but they are high-quality references for the modeling part.

# Recommended construction

For your course, I would use this combination:

**Main backbone:**
**MIT OCW 18.330 Introduction to Numerical Analysis**

**Alternative / Stanford alignment:**
**Stanford CME 108 / MATH 114 Introduction to Scientific Computing**

**For deeper theory:**
**MIT OCW 18.335J Introduction to Numerical Methods**

**For Тема 1 modeling:**
**MIT RES.15-004 System Dynamics** plus selected ideas from **MIT 2.003 / 2.004**

So the most honest answer is:

> There is probably no single Stanford/MIT public course that covers both “modeling in systems analysis” and all classical numerical methods exactly as listed. But **MIT 18.330** or **Stanford CME 108** cover Topics 2–6 very well, and Topic 1 should be supplemented from MIT system dynamics / systems modeling materials.

For your Ukrainian syllabus, the closest English title would be:

**Numerical Methods and Mathematical Modeling for Systems Analysis**
or
**Numerical Methods in Systems Analysis**.
